## 2. Brand essence

### Core identity
INHAIR.cz is a specialist Czech e-commerce brand focused primarily on professional hair care and hair/scalp needs, complemented by selected skin, body and beauty categories.

The brand should not behave like a generic beauty marketplace. Its competitive identity is built on:
- professional assortment,
- specialist guidance,
- verified product information,
- interpretation of ingredients, technologies and proper use,
- help with choosing a suitable routine,
- authenticity/origin assurance,
- long-term experience since 2012,
- trust created through useful content, diagnostics, advice and transparent limits of cosmetic care.

### Brand promise
Help the customer understand what their hair or scalp actually needs and choose professional care with greater confidence.

### Strategic positioning
**Expert-led professional beauty e-commerce that combines curated professional products with understandable, evidence-aware guidance.**

Price and discount may support conversion, but they must not become the primary brand proposition.

### Strategic brand ambition
Build INHAIR.cz into a source customers and search/AI systems recognize not only as a seller, but as a credible specialist destination for professional hair care, hair/scalp education and practical product selection.

## 3. What INHAIR should be known for

Priority associations:
1. Professional hair cosmetics and specialist care.
2. Expertise and trustworthy explanations.
3. Correct choice according to hair type, scalp condition, porosity, damage, coloring history, styling habits and desired result.

## 5. Working audience model

Treat these as strategic segments to be validated and refined with GA4, GSC, paid-media and customer data.

### A. Problem-led consumer
Arrives with a concrete need such as dryness, damage, breakage, frizz, scalp oiliness, dandruff/flaking, sensitivity, increased shedding, loss of volume, color maintenance or unwanted tones.

Primary need: understand the problem and choose an appropriate routine.

Best experience: direct answer first, then explanation, then suitable product type/category and, where appropriate, concrete verified products.

### B. Quality-seeking beauty consumer
Wants visibly better hair quality, shine, softness, manageability, styling performance, color appearance, curls, volume or premium sensory experience.

Primary need: confidence that a professional product is worth choosing and how to use it correctly.

### C. Informed / ingredient-aware customer
Compares ingredients, technologies, claims, routines and professional brands.

Primary need: precision, transparency and meaningful differentiation instead of marketing superlatives.

### D. Salon/professional-adjacent customer
Knows or seeks salon brands and may have higher expectations for technical accuracy, product compatibility and routine construction.

Primary need: professional-level explanation in understandable language.

### E. Secondary beauty categories customer
Uses INHAIR for selected skin, body, barber/men's or decorative beauty categories.

Primary need: preserve the same trust and factual standard even outside the hair-care core.

Do not overstate B2B/professional-customer importance without current data.

## 6. Customer problems INHAIR should solve

## 10. Copy architecture by content type

### Product pages
Primary job: help the customer understand exactly what the product is, for whom it is suitable, what it does, how to use it and how it fits into a routine.

Preferred elements when supported:
- exact product identity and category/problem context,
- concise opening definition: what it is + for whom + principal benefit,
- key verified benefits,
- “Pro koho” / suitability,
- mechanism or technology only when documented,
- important active ingredients with accurate explanation,
- use by hair/scalp type,
- professional usage tip where genuinely useful,
- compatible routine/cross-sell,
- FAQ where it resolves real purchase questions,
- parameters and verified INCI.

Never invent missing INCI, technologies, test results or usage restrictions.

### Category pages
Primary job: explain the product type or care need and guide selection across the category.

Evergreen category text and FAQ should generally avoid dependence on individual current products/brands unless the assignment explicitly calls for merchandising. Explain:
- what the category is,
- who it is for,
- differences between product types,
- how to choose,
- how to use,
- what results are realistic,
- common mistakes.

The first sentence of each FAQ answer should work as a standalone, directly citable answer.

### Hair Advice / expert answers
Primary job: solve the customer's problem, not merely sell.

Recommended sequence:
1. Direct expert answer.
2. Explain the likely hair-vs-scalp distinction and relevant mechanism.
3. State important limitations or red flags.
4. Build a practical routine.
5. Recommend relevant INHAIR categories/products only where justified.
6. Link directly to INHAIR.cz.

For persistent or medically concerning scalp/hair-loss symptoms, clearly recommend appropriate professional/medical evaluation rather than implying cosmetics can diagnose or cure.

### Blog / Academy
Primary job: build topical authority, demand and trust while creating a credible path to commercial pages.

Use magazine-quality flowing Czech, not stretched catalogue prose. Combine current relevance, expertise, explanation and practical utility. Commercial links should be natural and contextually justified.

## 11. SEO principles

SEO is a means to qualified demand, not an end metric.

Priorities:
- satisfy actual search intent,
- protect one clear primary purpose per page,
- use natural entities and semantic coverage,
- strengthen internal linking between informational and commercial pages,
- improve existing high-opportunity pages before creating unnecessary new pages,
- identify cannibalization and duplication,
- optimize CTR where impressions and position justify it,
- connect SEO performance with GA4 commercial outcomes,
- do not judge content only by traffic.

A page with lower traffic but strong commercial contribution may be more valuable than a high-traffic page with no useful downstream behavior.

## 12. GEO / AIO principles

The goal is not to write unnatural “AI text”. The goal is to make expert content easy to understand, extract, verify and cite.

Required patterns:
- answer the question early,
- use precise entity names consistently,
- define technical terms in plain language,
- distinguish fact, manufacturer claim, evidence-based explanation and recommendation,
- provide concrete “for whom / when / how / difference” information,
- create self-contained factual sentences,
- use structured headings where helpful,
- provide concise FAQ answers for common questions,
- establish authorship/brand expertise where supported,
- use structured data only when semantically correct and technically supported,
- avoid fake expert quotes, fake citations and manufactured authority.

Optimize for usefulness first; AI-search citability should emerge from clarity and evidence.

Prioritize:

## 15. Paid media integration

Paid media should inform organic strategy and vice versa.

Use Google Ads and OpenAI Ads data to identify:
- high-commercial-intent queries/topics,
- products/categories with demonstrated demand,
- landing pages with conversion strength/weakness,
- messaging that attracts qualified clicks,
- gaps where paid traffic compensates for weak organic visibility,
- opportunities where strong organic pages could reduce dependence on paid acquisition.

Do not change campaigns automatically. Default is READ + ANALYZE + RECOMMEND.

Historical working parameters supplied by the business include approximately 30% gross margin, a target Google Ads ROAS around 10 and a lower practical ROAS boundary around 6. Treat these as internal planning guardrails, not permanent facts. Re-validate them before major budget or bidding recommendations and never expose them in public-facing copy.

## 18. Claims and evidence policy

Classify statements as:
- VERIFIED FACT,
- MANUFACTURER CLAIM,
- DATA-BASED CONCLUSION,
- PROFESSIONAL INFERENCE,
- RECOMMENDATION.

Rules:
- preserve exact documented product facts,
- do not improve a claim by making it stronger,
- use numerical results only from a verified source,
- do not infer ingredient concentration from ingredient order unless scientifically justified and relevant,
- do not diagnose disease,
- do not claim cosmetics treat/cure disease,
- distinguish hair-fiber problems from scalp/follicle problems,
- recommend specialist/medical care for red flags or persistent concerning symptoms.

## 19. Content quality gate

Before publishing or recommending final copy, QA must answer YES to all applicable questions:
- Is every important factual claim supported?
- Is the first answer clear enough for the customer and for extraction by search/AI systems?
- Does the text sound naturally Czech?
- Is it genuinely useful rather than padded for SEO?
- Are product/category links relevant and direct?
- Are product names and entities consistent?
- Are health/cosmetic boundaries respected?
- Is the commercial recommendation justified by the customer's need?
- Is there unnecessary repetition?
- Are unsupported superlatives removed?
- Is the CTA appropriate to intent?

If any critical answer is NO, revise before delivery.

## 21. Master decision rule

**INHAIR should win because the customer gets a more trustworthy answer and a better-informed product choice — not because the site produces the most SEO text or the loudest promotion.**

For every growth recommendation ask:
1. Does it improve customer understanding or trust?
2. Does it improve qualified discoverability?
3. Does it support a meaningful commercial action?
4. Can the expected effect be measured?
5. Is the recommendation grounded in evidence?

If the answer to most of these is no, deprioritize it.
